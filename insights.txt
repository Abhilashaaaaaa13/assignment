## Trader Performance vs Market Sentiment — Summary

### Methodology
The analysis combines two datasets:  
(1) daily Bitcoin market sentiment (Fear/Greed) and  
(2) historical trader-level execution data from Hyperliquid.

All trades were aggregated at a **daily trader level** to compute key performance and behavior metrics, including:
- daily PnL per trader
- win rate
- number of trades per day
- average position size (leverage proxy)
- long/short bias

Trader metrics were then aligned with daily market sentiment and compared across **Fear vs Greed regimes**.  
Insights were validated using **summary tables and visualizations (boxplots and bar charts)**.  
No future information was used, ensuring the analysis reflects realistic trading conditions.



### Key Insights
1. **Performance differs by sentiment**  
   Traders achieve higher average daily PnL during Greed periods compared to Fear periods. However, PnL volatility is also higher during Greed, indicating increased drawdown risk.

2. **Trader behavior becomes more aggressive during Greed**  
   Both trade frequency and average position size increase during Greed days, suggesting higher risk appetite compared to Fear regimes.

3. **Long bias increases without proportional improvement in win rate**  
   Although traders exhibit a stronger long bias during Greed periods, the corresponding increase in win rate is marginal, indicating potential overconfidence rather than a genuine edge.

All insights are supported by corresponding tables and charts included in the notebook and saved in the `charts/` directory.



### Strategy Recommendations
1. Sentiment-aware risk management 
   Reduce leverage and position size during Fear periods to limit drawdowns, while allowing normal or slightly higher exposure during Greed periods.

2. Selective activity scaling
   Increase trade frequency only during Greed regimes and primarily for traders who already exhibit consistent performance, rather than uniformly increasing activity across all traders.

These sentiment-conditioned rules can improve risk-adjusted returns without requiring precise market timing.
